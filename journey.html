Step 1 of 5

Listen

Start by asking the people who will use it.

Before I decide what to build, I talk to people. At Phatse, the party marketplace I founded, I interviewed more than 100 customers and event organisers, and the roadmap came from what they told me. At K2 I spoke to learners after they bought a course, to hear why they bought it and where they got stuck.

I picked the habit up on cruise ships, running a 60-person dining team for P&O Cruises. Guests tell you quickly what works. Our NPS stayed above 80 on 85% or more of cruises.

Step 2 of 5

Decide

Choose what to build, and what to leave out.

A round of interviews gives you a long list. Deciding is cutting it down. For each new K2 course I worked out who it was for, checked what competitors charged, proposed a price and chose whether to build it in-house or with an outside expert, all within an approved budget. At Phatse the roadmap followed the interviews, and the business earned a 20% commission on each booking.

  • Who it is forDemand research and a defined target customer.
  • Who builds itIn-house, or an outside subject-matter expert.
  • PriceProposed from competitor analysis.

Step 3 of 5

Build

Write down what it should do, then work with the people who build it.

I write the spec and work alongside the engineers. At K2 I specified an AI workflow that reads Salesforce release notes and flags the course content that needs updating. My engineering team built it. I did the same with a learner progress dashboard, which helped lift course completion by 24%.

At Phatse I led a team of 18: 8 engineers, 5 in sales and marketing and 5 interns. Earlier, at SearchFinn, I worked with engineers to rebuild a web app as a React single-page app and cut its load time by 40%.

    Step 4 of 5

    Launch

    Ship it, then watch what the numbers say.

    At K2 I launched about 45 of the 57 courses myself. I published each one on LearnDash and wrote the marketing brief for the launch.

    At Phatse the marketplace booked ₹6 lakh in its first two months, about 85 orders at ₹7,000 each. I designed a referral reward that paid customers instantly when a friend booked, and it kept the cost of winning a customer near ₹600. Phatse was named among India's Top 25 startups at the BITS Pilani Entrepreneurship Conclave in 2021.

    Step 5 of 5

    Improve

    Launch day is the start of the work.

    Every Salesforce release changes something, so a course goes out of date unless someone keeps it current. I kept all 57 K2 courses current. The AI workflow cut update cycles from 80–90 days to 30–45, and the team needed fell from 5 people to 3. The learner dashboard helped lift completion by 24%.

    At Phatse, the referral feature drove 50% month-over-month growth and cut churn by 22%.
